# KIC - Innovative early diagnostics of Digestive Diseases

**Source:** https://www.nwo.nl/en/calls/kic-innovative-early-diagnostics-of-digestive-diseases

Gastrointestinal diseases are among the top three causes of death in the Netherlands and account for approximately 10% of total mortality. However, they are not always recognized or treated in a timely manner. This research programma aims to support interdisciplinary and transdisciplinary research into innovative or radically improved early diagnosis for gastrointestinal diseases with a high disease burden.

## Explore

### Purpose

Gastrointestinal (GI) diseases are among the top three causes of death in the Netherlands and account for approximately 10 per cent of total mortality. The impact of gastrointestinal diseases – a heterogeneous group comprising more than two hundred clinical conditions – on society is enormous. It is estimated that gastrointestinal diseases account for 3.8 per cent of total direct healthcare costs and for an even higher percentage of long-term sick leave.

The reasons for this lie in the increase in incidence and, because many of these diseases are chronic in nature, also in their prevalence. This increase is attributable to an ageing population, the Western lifestyle (Western diet, limited physical activity, insufficient or disrupted sleep and high levels of stress), but, notably, also to the fact that these diseases are occurring much earlier in life.

Another key cause is the often late diagnosis of disease or disease progression. This is related, amongst other things, to the often non-specific symptoms, but also to the fact that diagnostic methods within the diverse field of gastroenterology are still lacking or have limitations in terms of sensitivity, accessibility and/or speed. This programme focuses specifically on the early diagnosis of gastroenterological diseases in order to tackle this societal challenge.

### Who can apply

Proposals are to be submitted by the main applicant and one or more co-applicants on behalf of the consortium. An application is prepared by a consortium, which includes the applicants and other participants.

There are four categories of participants within a consortium:

1. main applicant
2. co-applicant(s)
3. co-funder(s)
4. cooperation partner(s)
A consortium must comprise at least a main applicant, a co-applicant and two co-funders, as well as an expert by experience or a representative of a patients’ organisation, possibly supplemented by one or more cooperation partners.

### Consortium building

To increase the research’s potential for impact, collaboration is essential between researchers, with researchers from other disciplines, and with societal partners, such as companies and social organisations. In this call for proposals, NWO supports consortium formation by organising an online matchmaking event.

### What to apply for

In this Call for Proposals, it is possible to apply for a grant with a minimum of €530,000 and a maximum of €679,000 in NWO funding. NWO will fund up to 80 per cent of the total project scope; the remainder of the project budget will be contributed through mandatory co-funding.

### Assessment

### Criteria

Proposals submitted under this Call for Proposals are assessed on their merits against the following criteria:

- Problem definition and analysis (25%)
- Envisaged impact and route to impact (25%)
- Quality of the consortium (30%)
- Quality of the research  (20%)

### Procedure

The assessment procedure for a proposal consists of the following steps (see timeline in subsection 3.1):

- submission of a letter of intent;
- matchmaking and information session
- submission of the proposal
- preliminary advice from the assessment committee;
- selection interview;
- interview;
- assessment committee meeting;
- decision-making.
An external independent assessment committee will be appointed by the NWO Executive Board for this round. Members are drawn from the scientific community and wider society and possess expertise in the relevant field. It is the assessment committee’s task to evaluate the proposals received against the assessment criteria. Each proposal is assessed on its own merits.

### Write proposal

This Call for Proposals aims to establish [inter- and transdisciplinary](https://www.nwo.nl/onderzoeksprogrammas/kennis-en-innovatieconvenant-kic/kic-2020-2023/interdisciplinaire-samenwerking-binnen-het-kennis-en-innovatieconvenant-2020-2023#interdisciplinaire-samenwerking-binnen-het-kic) consortia. This is necessary to ensure sufficient co-creation, to properly embed the impact of the intended research results on quality of life, and to gain insight into and incorporate patient preferences and needs. Interdisciplinary cooperation refers to research in which knowledge and expertise from different scientific disciplines are integrated from the outset in order to solve problems and explain phenomena together. It involves cooperation between the humanities, natural sciences and social sciences. Transdisciplinary research involves the knowledge and expertise of societal actors and the business sector, such as patients and patient organisations, societal partners and companies, as well as interregional cooperation.

Research aimed at addressing the knowledge gaps described in this partnership programme, for example, utilises infrastructure in the fields of technology, data and healthcare, and focuses on future implementation and upscaling in clinical practice.
