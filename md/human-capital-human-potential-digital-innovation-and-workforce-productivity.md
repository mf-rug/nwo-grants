# Human Capital – Human Potential: Digital innovation and workforce productivity

**Source:** https://www.nwo.nl/en/calls/human-capital-human-potential-digital-innovation-and-workforce-productivity

With this call, we invite parties to form a consortium to conduct research into the factors influencing the adoption of AI in SMEs, the empirical evidence on how technological adoption lead to improved quality of work and productivity growth, and the decision-making processes surrounding the implementation of technology by employees within SMEs.

## Explore

### Purpose

This Call for Proposals aims to contribute to improving the quality of work, workforce productivity, and innovation capacity within small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in the Netherlands. On the one hand, it focusses on how applications involving artificial intelligence (AI) and human-robot collaboration (robotics) can effectively help reduce work pressure, foster greater job satisfaction, and increase productivity. For instance, by providing support for administrative, organizational, and operational tasks. On the other hand, it addresses how these innovative technologies can enhance business processes, thereby strengthening the Netherlands’ economic position in the long term. To this end, researchers are invited to submit proposals developed in collaboration with SMEs and relevant societal organisations.

### Who can apply

Applications are submitted by a main applicant and one or more co-applicants. An application is prepared by a consortium, which includes other participants in addition to the applicants. Four categories of participants in a consortium are distinguished:

1. Main applicant
2. Co-applicant(s)
3. Co-funder(s)
4. Cooperation partner(s)
All participants in the consortium must play an active role in formulating the research questions and in the design and implementation of the project.

### Consortium building

For this Call for proposals, NWO organises a matchmaking event on the 14th of January at the NWO office in Utrecht.

### Assessment

### Criteria

Proposals will be assessed by the following four criteria:

1. Problem definition and analysis (20%)
2. Envisaged impact and route to impact (20%)
3. Quality of the consortium (30%)
4. Quality of the research (30%)

### Procedure

The application procedure consists of the following steps:

- matchmaking;
- submission of the proposal;
- admissibility of the proposal;
- pre-advice assessment committee;
- interview selection (optional);
- interview;
- assessment committee meeting;
- decision-making.

### Write proposal

The following conditions for submission will be assessed by NWO:

- the main applicant has participated in the matchmaking event;
- the application is submitted before the set deadline;
- the main applicant and co-applicant(s) meet the conditions stated in Section 3.2 and annex 7.1.3;
- the applying consortium contains two or more co-funders;
- the co-funders meet the conditions stated in Section 3.6.1;
- the application complies with the DORA guidelines as described in Section 4.3.4;
- the application has been completed and submitted using the forms made available in ISAAC and on the funding page for this round on the NWO website;
- the data management section has been completed (in which applicants specify how data resulting from the research will be handled);
- the application form and the mandatory annexes have been completed correctly, in full, and in accordance with the instructions;
- the application has been submitted via the principal applicant via the main applicant’s ISAAC account;
- the application is written in English;
- the application complies with the requirements regarding page or word limits;
- the budget in the application has been prepared in accordance with the terms of this Call for proposals (using the provided template, which includes the most up-to-date rates);
- the required co-funding has been correctly and full committed – following any potential request for supplementation or amendment – by means of co-funding statements;
the proposed project has a maximum duration of 6 years.
